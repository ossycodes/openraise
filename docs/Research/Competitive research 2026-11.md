# Competitive research 2026-11

The Nigerian donation-crowdfunding market as of 2026-11-05. Figures are each
platform's own claims from its website, not independently verified.

## Gathr (gathrhq.com)

The reference product for parity.

- Gathr Technologies Ltd (CAC RC-9619904). Founder/CEO Olanrewaju
  Sule-balogun, co-founder/CTO Ridwan Kasim. Launched **August 2026**.
- Campaigns for medical, education, emergencies, funerals, business, community,
  creative, sports, travel and more (16 categories).
- Creators verified with NIN (QoreID / VerifyMe) before going live.
- Donations in USD (cards, 130+ countries), NGN (card, transfer, USSD), GHS and
  KES (mobile money). Payments through **Bachs**.
- Payouts on demand to a Nigerian bank account "within 1 hour", without waiting
  for the campaign to end; the creator keeps funds even if the goal isn't met.
- AI campaign builder (writes description + story), AI-assisted social posts.
- Creator updates to donors; guides and SEO landing pages per cause.
- **Fees:** 3.5% platform fee per donation; donor pays 1.5% + ₦100 (flat fee
  only above ₦2,500); a separate withdrawal fee, amount not published.
- Traction is early: largest featured campaign ₦3.35M from 64 donors; most have
  0–3 donors.

## Crowdr (oncrowdr.com)

The incumbent and main rival.

- Started 2024. Claims ₦700M+ raised, 60K+ users, 6,000+ campaigns. Partners
  include AIESEC, EiE and Bethesda School for the Blind. No outside funding
  listed on Tracxn.
- **Fees:** 5% platform fee; donor pays 1.5% + ₦100. Gathr's "others charge 5%"
  is aimed at Crowdr.
- Extras: recurring donations, NGO volunteer matching, CSR tools.

The largest competitor is informal: account numbers posted on X and WhatsApp.
It costs nothing but gives donors no proof of where money went.

## What parity means for OpenRaise

Everything in Gathr's list plus Crowdr's extras: categories, NIN-verified
creators, multi-currency donations, on-demand payouts, AI story writer and
social posts, updates, recurring donations, organisations, volunteers, CSR.

## Where an open-source platform can do better

- **Self-hosting.** NGOs, churches and associations can run their own instance
  with their own fees (including 0%) and their own payment account.
- **Public, auditable ledger.** Every campaign can show a verifiable money
  trail: raised, fees, withdrawals. This answers the main objection to giving
  online in Nigeria and the main weakness of informal giving.
- **Published fees, all of them.** Including the withdrawal fee Gathr leaves
  out.
- **Provider choice.** Paystack, Bachs and later Flutterwave, so a deployment
  isn't hostage to one processor.
